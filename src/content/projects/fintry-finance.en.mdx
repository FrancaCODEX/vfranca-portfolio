## The problem

Small businesses often manage their finances in scattered spreadsheets: payables, receivables, sales and inventory don't talk to each other, and nobody really knows what next month's cash position will look like.

**Fintry** brings all of that into a single application, with multiple companies, teams and **access roles** (Admin, Finance and Read-only).

## What already works

- **Finance:** accounts with computed balances, income and expenses, installments and recurring entries (2 to 120 occurrences), partial payments and atomic transfers between accounts
- **Projected daily cash flow** that flags negative balances, plus a simulator that compares scenarios without touching real records
- **Sales, inventory, services and proposals:** approving a proposal creates the sale and the income in a single transaction
- **Bank reconciliation:** OFX statement import with a preview and a decision per movement (link, create an entry, or ignore with a reason)
- **Asaas billing integration:** Pix/boleto, sandbox, status lookup and confirmation **webhooks**
- **Category budgets**, a pending-items center and reports exportable to **CSV, Excel and PDF**
- **Team and security:** expiring invites, single-use password recovery, session invalidation on password change and a full **change history** (audit trail)

## Architecture

The backend is a **modular monolith** in Spring Boot. Each domain is a module with its own controller, service and repository layers, and the React UI is served by the backend itself.

```text
React (TypeScript, Vite)
        │  HTTP + session
        ▼
Spring Security ── roles: ADMIN · FINANCE · READ-ONLY
        │
        ▼
REST Controllers  /api/v1/...
        │
        ▼
Services (financial rules, @Transactional)
        │
        ▼
JPA Repositories ──▶ PostgreSQL 17   (Flyway migrations)
        │
        └──▶ Asaas (Pix/boleto)  ◀── webhook
```

## Technical decisions

### Per-company isolation

The company behind each operation is **derived from the authenticated session**, never from a client-sent parameter. A user can't see another company's data, even by requesting an ID directly. The same applies to uploaded receipts (PDF and images).

### Consistent financial operations

Confirming a sale re-checks inventory, records stock movements and creates a single linked income entry. If any step fails, **the whole confirmation is rolled back**. Confirming again is idempotent: no second income entry and no second stock movement.

### Preserved history

Settled entries are never edited. To fix one, it's cancelled and recorded again, so balances and the audit trail always stay explainable.
